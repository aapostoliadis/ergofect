C

Evidence before action

Make every proposed change reviewable.

Illustrative revision path
Approved baselineScope · version · plan
→
New evidenceRevision · comment · finding

Revision impact

Compare the new information with the approved baseline. Identify affected deliverables, effort, cost, dates, and planning dependencies. Keep uncertainty explicit.

The review packet
  • Source and version behind the proposed change
  • What changed, what is affected, and what remains unknown
  • Suggested response or scope variation
  • Named reviewer, decision, and approval record

Accept, return for clarification, or reject. Update the baseline only after approval.

D

Governed follow-through

Close the loop. Keep the decision visible.

↻

Follow-on survey

Prepare the next survey from agreed triggers and open questions. Add findings to the evidence record and flag any effect on scope or plan.

Trigger → findings → impact review

A person approves the survey and any resulting commitment.

↻

Comment responses

Connect each comment to its source and revision. Draft a response, identify unresolved points, and retain the approved answer.

Comment → draft → reviewed response

A named reviewer approves external responses before sending.

↻

Scope variations

Turn a change request into a proposed variation with its scope, cost, schedule, and dependency impact.

Request → impact → approved variation

Commercial approval precedes a revised scope or schedule.

Each loop writes back to the shared evidence record and reopens the relevant review gate. Triggers and permissions are agreed for the engagement.

E

People steer the work

Measure the handoff. Own the outcome.

Define the boundariesAgree sources, access, rules, reviewers, and escalation paths before a workflow runs.
Review the exceptionsResolve missing evidence and conflicting instructions. Approve the commitments that change the work.
Measure against a baselineTrack turnaround time, rework, unresolved items, and review effort. Validate improvement with delivery evidence.

Illustrative operating model. Capabilities, integrations, automation boundaries, and review responsibilities are configured and agreed for each engagement. This blueprint is not a claim that every capability is deployed for every client.
